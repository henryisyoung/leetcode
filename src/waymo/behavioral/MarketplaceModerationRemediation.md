# UGC Marketplace Moderation: Remediation Instead of Takedown

**Behavioral story (STAR)** — themes: leadership by reframing, balancing safety vs. business, cross-functional influence, customer/creator empathy.

> **Maps to questions like:** "Tell me about a time you had to balance competing priorities," "a time you challenged the status quo," "a time you influenced without authority," "a hard tradeoff between two stakeholders," "a time you reframed a problem."

---

## Situation

At Roblox I was a **technical lead for moderation systems** on the **UGC Avatar Marketplace**. A recurring tension: **marketplace growth vs. platform safety**.

When content violated policy, the default was blunt — **take the item down and refund all purchasers**. But many violations came from **top creators with large purchaser bases**, so takedowns protected the platform while creating real **business impact, creator frustration, and operational overhead**.

## Task

I owned finding a better answer than the binary **"leave it up" vs. "take it down."** I needed a solution that **held the safety bar** while reducing disruption — and that required aligning **Avatar Marketplace, Moderation Operations, and Safety** partners who had different priorities.

## Action

Instead of accepting the binary, I drove a **reframe**: give creators a **second chance via a remediation workflow**.

- If a creator's content violated policy, they could **submit a corrected version**.
- The new version went through **moderation + validation review**.
- If it complied, it **replaced the original** — no full takedown-and-refund cycle.

I worked across the three orgs to align on the policy guardrails (what's remediable vs. hard-takedown), the review flow, and the state handling for the swap.

## Result

- **Preserved the platform's safety standards** while minimizing disruption to creators and purchasers.
- Created **stronger incentives for creators to fix issues** rather than lose content entirely.
- Turned a zero-sum safety-vs-business fight into a **positive-sum** outcome.

## The lesson (say this out loud)

> "Leadership is often about **reframing the problem**. Instead of forcing a tradeoff between safety and business goals, we designed a path that improved both."

## Variant: "Talk about feedback you got" question

Same story, but the spotlight shifts **from the design to you receiving and acting on feedback**. This question tests: are you coachable, non-defensive, and do you actually change? The remediation workflow becomes the **evidence** you acted, not the point.

**The feedback is the trigger, not the solution.** Make the feedback-giver a *person* (a Marketplace PM / your manager / a partner lead), not a faceless team.

> **Situation / the feedback:** "As a moderation tech lead my instinct was safety-first — when content violated policy, take it down and refund. I got pointed feedback from a Marketplace PM and from creators that I was optimizing for safety in isolation and ignoring business and creator impact — treating it as a binary when the org needed something more nuanced."
>
> **How I took it (the honest part):** "My first reaction was defensive — these were real policy violations. But sitting with it, I realized the feedback wasn't 'lower the safety bar,' it was 'you haven't explored the solution space.' That gap was on me."
>
> **Action:** "I went back and designed the remediation workflow — second chance, re-moderation, swap-on-compliance — which held the safety bar *and* addressed their concern."
>
> **Result:** "Preserved safety, cut disruption — and the bigger takeaway: I now pull in the stakeholders who'll disagree with me *before* I lock a design, because that feedback made the solution materially better."

**Delivery cautions:**
- Show a real arc: a flicker of defensiveness → reflection → change. Instant agreement reads as fake.
- Name a single person who gave the feedback; feedback questions want a human moment.
- End on the **behavior change**, not the feature.

## Talking points / likely follow-ups

- **Where's the line?** Not everything is remediable — egregious violations still get hard takedowns + refunds. Remediation is for fixable policy issues.
- **Abuse risk:** prevent creators from gaming "submit a clean version then revert" — versioning, re-moderation on every change, limited remediation attempts.
- **Influence without authority:** the win was aligning Safety (won't lower the bar), Marketplace (wants growth), and Mod Ops (wants less manual churn) on one workflow.
- **Waymo bridge:** same instinct applies to safety-critical tradeoffs — resist false binaries (ship vs. block) and design a **third option** that keeps the safety guarantee intact while reducing operational/business cost.
